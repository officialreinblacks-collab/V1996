ICT Playbook Indicator

Draws the full ICT story on your chart: liquidity gets taken, structure shifts, then a trade box with stop, target and the levels to watch.

Where to find it: Indicators → Trend → ICT Playbook. Tap the indicator's settings and the 📖 Read script button at the top of Inputs opens this page.

What it does

ICT Playbook looks for one specific story and draws every step of it so you can read it at a glance:

Price builds liquidity (a tight sideways box, or a clear swing high or low) → price grabs that liquidity and snaps back → the market makes a Market Structure Shift (MSS) → a trade box appears with entry, stop and target. Around that story it marks the gaps, order blocks and levels that usually matter next.

Both directions are covered. A sell setup starts with buyside liquidity taken above. A buy setup is the exact mirror and starts with sellside liquidity taken below.

Quick start

  1. Open a liquid market on 5m, 15m or 1h. Scalping and intraday timeframes suit it best.
  2. Add ICT Playbook from Indicators → Trend. By default it shows the latest 2 setups, so the chart stays clean.
  3. Wait for the MSS label and the red/green trade box. That is the moment the setup is complete.
  4. Read the extra levels (-FVG, -OB, -IOFED, HTF block) to decide where you want to enter. The box entry is the MSS close, the levels give you better prices if price comes back.
  5. Check the bigger picture on a higher timeframe before you act. The indicator shows structure, not the news.

How one setup is built

1 · Liquidity pool

Two things count as liquidity. A Liquidity Generation box: a sideways range at least Liquidity box min bars long and not taller than Liquidity box max height × ATR. And a swing high / low, found with Swing length bars on each side (you can turn swings off).

The pool line is labelled Buyside Liquidity (above, for sells) or Sellside Liquidity (below, for buys).

2 · Liquidity Grab

Price pushes through the pool by a meaningful amount, then a candle closes back inside within two candles. A small arc and the words Liquidity Grab mark the spike. The lowest point (or highest, for buys) of the build-up is marked as Accumulation Low / High.

3 · MSS (market structure shift)

Before the grab, the indicator remembers the last swing in the opposite direction. When a candle closes through that swing inside the MSS search window (and before price makes a new extreme beyond the grab), the dashed MSS line is drawn. If it never closes through in time, there is no setup.

4 · Trade box

Entry is the close of the MSS candle. Stop sits just beyond the grab extreme. Target is the risk multiplied by your Reward : risk (default 1:3). Red = risk, green = reward. The label shows 1:3 while the trade is open and adds TP or SL once price has hit one of them.

Good to know

  • Setups are built from closed candles. The candle still forming is ignored, so a drawn setup does not vanish when it closes.
  • After a setup completes, the next setup on the same side has to start with a new liquidity grab after that MSS.

  • Box height, exhaustion distance and stops are measured in ATR, so they scale by themselves between markets.
Important: this is a chart-reading aid, not a promise of profit. Every setup can fail and hit its stop. Test it on a demo or on past candles first and risk only what you can afford to lose.

ICT Imbalances [VHX] Indicator

Marks the places where price moved so fast that one side had little or no trading: Volume Imbalances, Gaps and Fair Value Gaps. Each box stays on the chart until price comes back and fills it.

Where to find it: Indicators → Trend → ICT · GAPS, Volume & Price Imbalances [VHX]. Tap the indicator's settings and the 📖 Read script button at the top of Inputs opens this page.

What it does

Traders watch these boxes because price often returns to them before moving on. The indicator finds five kinds of imbalance and draws each as a coloured box that stretches to the right while it is still unfilled:

MarkMeaning
VI+ / VI-Volume Imbalance. Two candles of the same colour whose bodies do not overlap, while their wicks still touch. The box is the empty space between the two bodies.
GAP+ / GAP-True gap. The new candle's low is above the last candle's high (or its high is below the last low). No trading happened in that zone at all.
FVG+ / FVG-Fair Value Gap. A three candle pattern where the first and third candle leave a hole that the middle candle did not cover.
LV+ / LV-Liquidity Void. Several FVGs in a row in the same direction, joined into one tall box.
Imp.FVG+ / Imp.FVG-Implied FVG. A softer gap built from the candle bodies and wicks when the three candles overlap a little.
I.FVG+ / I.FVG-Inverse FVG. An FVG that price closed through. It flips role: a broken bullish FVG now acts as resistance, a broken bearish FVG as support.
Dashed lineC.E. (Consequent Encroachment). The middle of the box, a level price often reacts to.

Green boxes are bullish (support below price). Red boxes are bearish (resistance above price). Gaps are sky blue and inverse FVGs are amber.

Quick start

  1. Open any market and add ICT Imbalances [VHX] from Indicators → Trend.
  2. Leave the defaults on first: Classic Volume Imbalances, True GAPs and Normal FVGs.
  3. Look for boxes that are still stretching to the right. Those are unfilled and still active.
  4. When price returns into a box, it lights up (highlight). Watch the dashed C.E. line for a reaction.
  5. When a candle closes through a box, it turns grey and stops. That imbalance is done.

How a box ends

Engulf (default)

A bullish box ends when a candle body closes below it. A bearish box ends when a body closes above it. Wick touches do not end the box, they only highlight it.

Mitigate

The box ends as soon as price touches it with a wick. The strictest and fastest way to retire a box.

Rebalance (FVG and GAP only)

The box shrinks as price fills it. Only the part that is still empty stays on the chart. When price crosses the whole box, it ends.

Time and Price Lines and Zones Indicator

Time and price are two things traders watch all day: when something happens and where price is. This indicator lets you type what you want to see, in plain words, and it draws the lines and zones on the chart. There is no limit to how many you add.

Where to find it: Indicators → Trend → Time and Price Lines and Zones. Tap the indicator's settings and the 📖 Read script button at the top of Inputs opens this page. The big text box under it is where you type your instructions.

How it works

Each new line in the text box makes one line, a set of lines, or a price zone inside a time window. Say the times, the price levels (open, high, low, close) and the look (color, line style, thickness, shade, extend). The indicator reads the line and plots it on every day shown.

Keep each line simple. This is a typed tool, so it understands short, straightforward instructions. One idea per line works best.

Quick start

  1. Add the indicator. The text box already holds a working example (Asia, London and New York kill zones, an ORB zone, a midnight line and a 9:30 high and low).
  2. Check Time zone in the settings. Times you type are read in that zone. The default is America/New_York.
  3. Change a time or a color in the text box and tap OK. The chart updates at once.
  4. To hide a line without deleting it, put # in front of it.

What you can type

Time (always required)

Hours are 0 to 23 (0 is midnight) and minutes are 0 to 59. Type one time for a single moment, or two times to box a period.

Type shade, shaded, fill or filled to color the area between two times or two price levels with a see-through color. The word zone also fills. Leave them out for an empty box.

Extend or not

Type extend or extended to stretch a zone to the end of the trading day without tracking new price changes. This is how an Open Range Breakout (ORB) is drawn: the high and low of 9:30 to 10:00 stay fixed and run to the right.

ICT Structure Engine Indicator + Strategy

Reads the market the way ICT traders do: structure breaks, order blocks, fair value gaps, liquidity sweeps, killzones and the daily bias. When a full story lines up (sweep → CHoCH → retest) it marks a BUY or SELL with a stop loss and three targets.

Where to find it: Indicators → Trend → ICT Structure Engine. Tap the indicator's settings and the 📖 Read script button at the top of Inputs opens this page. Best on 5m, 15m or 1H charts. It uses closed candles only, so nothing repaints.

What you see on the chart

MarkMeaning
BOS / CHoCHBreak of structure continues the trend. Change of character is the first break against it. Solid lines are external structure, dashed lines are internal.
HH / LH / HL / LLConfirmed swing points. A label appears a few candles after its swing (confirmation lag).
Bull OB / Bear OB ★Order block with a score of 0 to 4 stars: +1 FVG in the move, +1 liquidity sweep at the start, +1 same direction as the higher timeframe, +1 in discount (demand) or premium (supply). The dotted line is the 50%.
BreakerAn order block that was closed through. It flips side and can act as the opposite zone. Maximum 2 stars.
FVGFair value gap. Blue is bullish, orange is bearish. It shrinks as price fills it and disappears when it is fully filled.
RB / PBRejection block (long wick of a swing) and propulsion block (candle that tapped an order block, then price closed beyond it).
EQH / EQL / SweepEqual highs / lows are liquidity. Sweep means price poked through, then closed back (stops taken).
PDH PDL PWH PWLPrevious day and previous week high / low. Session highs and lows (Asia H, London L…) become levels when the session ends.
Killzone boxesAsian, London, NY AM and NY PM ranges (up to 1H charts).
Midnight Open + BiasAmber dashed line at 00:00 New York with the daily bias arrow.
Judas ▲ / ▼A false move against the daily bias past the midnight open, then a close back across it.
Premium / Discount / OTEThe current range split at 50%. The OTE band is the 62–79% retracement in the trend direction.
Setup ▲ / ▼A setup is armed and waiting for the first retest of its zone.
▲ BUY / ▼ SELLTrade entry at the close of the retest candle. Lines show entry, SL, TP1, TP2 and TP3.

The setup in four steps

  1. Sweep. Price takes a liquidity level (equal highs / lows, a swing, PDH / PDL, a session high / low) and closes back.
  2. CHoCH. Within the window (default 20 candles) price breaks structure the other way, and the leg left a fair value gap (displacement).
  3. Wait. The fair value gap (or order block) of that move is the entry zone. It waits up to 30 candles for the first return.
  4. Entry. The candle that touches the zone and does not close beyond it is the signal. The entry location filter (discount for longs, premium for shorts) must agree.

A setup is cancelled when it expires, when an opposite CHoCH appears, or when its zone breaks. The reason is shown in the alert.

Risk and targets

SettingOptions
Stop loss modeWick-Anchored (beyond the signal candle, default), Zone-Anchored (beyond the zone), Sweep-Anchored (beyond the sweep extreme, widest), ATR. Structure modes add 0.25 × ATR and keep at least 0.5 × ATR.
Take profit modeR-Multiples (default) uses 1R / 2R / 3R. Liquidity sends TP2 and TP3 to the next untaken liquidity. Std Deviations uses projections of the sweep → CHoCH leg (−1, −2, −2.5 SD).
Risk presetConservative, Balanced, Aggressive, Scalping or Custom.
Break-evenAfter TP1 the stop moves to entry. A trade counts as a win once TP1 is touched.

Dashboard

The panel shows trend, HTF bias, range position, the last signal, nearest demand and supply, setup status, killzone, daily bias, Judas, the open trade (entry, SL, TP1–3, R:R) and statistics (trades, wins, losses, win rate, form). Choose 24 Hours, 30 Days or All-Time for the statistics. They are recalculated from the loaded candles and are not a real backtest: the model assumes TP is touched before SL when both happen in the same candle.

Alerts

While the app is open you get a message when the event candle closes: entries, TP hits, break-even, SL, reversal, BOS / CHoCH, setup armed or cancelled, sweeps, order block touches, new FVG, RB / PB, Judas and daily bias. Each group can be turned off in Inputs.

If something does not show

  • A red message in the top left names a setting that is not valid (for example SD levels written with letters, or TP1 not smaller than TP2).
  • Killzones, midnight open, daily bias, Judas and PDH / PDL need an intraday chart. Killzone boxes and Judas work up to 1H.
  • The midnight open needs a candle that opens exactly at 00:00 in the chosen timezone. Without it the Judas swing is not checked that day.
  • Few or no BUY / SELL marks is normal: the setup is strict. To see more, set the time filter to Off, the entry location filter to Off, or turn off “Require displacement”.
  • If the higher timeframe is not above the chart timeframe it is ignored.
Important: this is a chart-reading aid, not a promise of profit. Every setup can fail and hit its stop. Test it on a demo or on past candles first and risk only what you can afford to lose.

HTF Fair Value Gap [LuxAlgo] Indicator

A Fair Value Gap (FVG) is a gap left between three candles, where price moved so fast that one side of the market was left behind. This indicator finds the latest FVG on a higher timeframe (Daily by default) and shows it on your lower timeframe chart, so you can trade the small chart with the big picture in view.

Where to find it: Indicators → Trend → HTF Fair Value Gap [LuxAlgo]. Tap the indicator's settings and the 📖 Read script button at the top of Inputs opens this page.

What you see

On the chartMeaning
Box to the rightThe latest higher timeframe FVG. Green is bullish (price jumped up), red is bearish (price dropped). The line through the middle is the average (the middle of the gap).
Wicks on the boxA thin line above and below the box shows how far price has pushed past the gap since it formed.
Dashed linesThe top and the bottom of the gap, drawn from the exact candle that made each edge and carried across to the box.
Box outlinePresent while the gap is Unmitigated. It disappears once the gap is Mitigated.
Shaded candlesThe candle where a new higher timeframe FVG showed up (you can turn this off).
DashboardShows Unmitigated or Mitigated. Bullish gap: mitigated when price closes below the bottom of the gap. Bearish gap: mitigated when price closes above the top.

Quick start

  1. Open a chart on a timeframe lower than the one you want (for example 1h or 15m for Daily).
  2. Add the indicator. The Daily FVG appears as a box on the right.
  3. Change Timeframe to 4h, 1d or 1w to watch a different level.
  4. Use Box offset and Box width to move or resize the box.

Settings

Bullish / Bearish FVGGreen / RedThe two colors.

How to use it

  • An Unmitigated gap is still open. Many traders watch it as an area where price may return and react.
  • The average line (middle of the gap) is a common level to watch.
  • A Mitigated gap has been closed through by price and is usually treated as used up.

If you see something odd

  • Red message “Chart timeframe needs to be lower…”: switch your chart to a timeframe below the one in the Timeframe setting, or raise the setting.
  • Dashboard says “No FVG”: there is no gap in the loaded history for that timeframe. Load more candles or pick a lower timeframe.
  • Daily and weekly candles are built from closed chart candles, using UTC days and weeks (weeks start on Monday). Only closed candles are used, so the box does not repaint.
Important: this is a chart-reading aid, not a promise of profit. Price often ignores gaps. Test it on a demo or on past candles first and risk only what you can afford to lose.

HTF Inversion Fair Value Gap | Flux Charts Indicator

A Fair Value Gap (FVG) is a gap left between three candles. When price later breaks back through that gap, the gap is said to be inverted (an IFVG): a broken bullish gap turns into a bearish zone and a broken bearish gap turns into a bullish zone. This indicator finds these on a higher timeframe (4h by default) and shows the newest one on your lower timeframe chart.

Where to find it: Indicators → Trend → HTF IFVG | Flux Charts. Tap the indicator's settings and the 📖 Read script button at the top of Inputs opens this page.

What you see

On the chartMeaning
Box to the rightThe newest inverted gap. Red = bearish IFVG (a bullish gap that was broken down). Green = bullish IFVG (a bearish gap that was broken up). It is split into two halves; the line between them is the middle of the gap.
Box outlinePresent while the IFVG is Valid. It disappears once the IFVG is Mitigated and a single line is drawn through the middle instead.
Wicks on the boxThin lines above and below the box show how far price has travelled past the gap since it was inverted.
Dashed linesThe top and bottom of the gap, drawn from the candle that made each edge, plus a middle line that starts at the candle that broke the gap.
DashboardShows Valid, Mitigated or None (no inverted gap found).

Quick start

  1. Open a chart on a timeframe lower than the one you want (for example 15m or 1h for 4h).
  2. Add the indicator. The newest 4h IFVG appears as a box on the right.
  3. Change Higher Timeframe to 1h, 1d or 1w to watch a different level.
  4. Use Offset and Width to move or resize the box.

Hammer Candlestick Strategy Strategy

Finds the setup from the HAMMER CANDLESTICK picture: price falls along a downtrend line, a hammer prints at support, and a candle that closes above the trendline gives the entry. The stop loss sits under the hammer, the target at the prior swing high, and the tool draws the green reward box and the red risk box for you.

Where to find it: Indicators → Trend → Hammer Candlestick Strategy. Tap its settings and the 📖 Read script button at the top of Inputs opens this page.

How the setup is found

  1. Downtrend line. The tool joins two lower swing highs (trendline rejection, resistance rejection). The line is only valid if no candle closed above it before the hammer.
  2. Hammer at support. A small body at the top of the candle and a long lower wick, after a fall, at the lowest low of the look-back or at an earlier swing low (support rejection).
  3. Entry. A green candle closes above the trendline (and above the hammer high) within the allowed number of bars. Entry price is that candle's close.
  4. Stop loss goes a little under the hammer low. Target is the highest high before the hammer (or a fixed Risk:Reward).
  5. Result. The boxes run until price touches the target (win) or the stop (loss). If both happen on one candle it counts as a loss.

Higher Timeframe Trend Frontier Indicator

A smooth trend line that lives inside each higher-timeframe candle, with the candle's range, its opening zone, and a map of where buyers and sellers are most likely trapped.

Where to find it: Indicators → Trend → Higher Timeframe Trend Frontier. Tap the indicator's settings and the 📖 Read script button at the top of Inputs opens this page.

What you see on the chart

On the chartMeaning
Echo FrontierThe main line. It trails price inside the current higher-timeframe candle and restarts at that candle's open. Green = rising, red = falling. The thick band behind it fades as the period ages.
Period dotA small dot where the frontier starts again after a new higher-timeframe candle opens.
HTF boxesOne shaded box per higher-timeframe candle, from its high to its low. Green means price is above that candle's open, red means below.
High / low labelsPrice tags on the exact candle that made each period's high and low.
ATR opening zoneA thin blue band centred on the period's open with a dotted line through the middle. Its half-width is a share of the previous higher-timeframe candle's ATR.
Vertical dividersDotted lines where each new higher-timeframe period begins.

Status textTop of the chart: the higher timeframe, how far through the period you are, and the current frontier speed multiplier.

How it works

The frontier follows price, but not blindly

On every candle the frontier moves toward the close by a limited step: Frontier Speed times the chart's ATR(14). A bigger speed hugs price, a smaller one is smoother.

Echo memory

The previous higher-timeframe candle's range (plus a quarter above and below) is cut into nine zones. Each time price sits in a zone, the tool remembers what price did three candles later, and old memories fade. When price is back in a zone that usually led higher, the frontier rises a little faster than it falls, and the other way round. Until enough history exists it moves evenly.

Late-period acceleration

Near the end of the higher-timeframe candle the speed rises from 1x to your final multiplier, so the frontier catches up with price before the period closes.

Trapped buyers and sellers

The three newest higher-timeframe candles (including the one still forming) are split into price rows. Each chart candle adds weight to the rows it touched, using volume, body, close position and wicks. Rows now under water count more: buyers who bought above the current price and sellers who sold below it. Red higher-timeframe candles lean toward trapped buyers, green ones toward trapped sellers, and older periods count a little less.

Estimate, not exchange data. The profile is built from candle open, high, low, close and volume. It is not real bid and ask delta, so treat it as a map of likely pressure.

Quick start

  1. Add Higher Timeframe Trend Frontier from Indicators → Trend.
  2. Pick a Higher timeframe above the chart timeframe (for example 1h or 4h on a 5m or 15m chart). If it is not higher, the chart shows a message saying so.
  3. Read the frontier colour: green and rising means buyers lead inside this period, red and falling means sellers lead.
  4. Check the open: price holding above the period's open and opening zone is a stronger bullish read. Price stuck inside the zone means the period has not picked a side.
  5. Look right for the blue (buyers) and orange (sellers) peaks. Price often reacts when it returns to those rows.
  6. Wait for the candle to close, then confirm with your own entry rules and stop loss.

Ways to use it

  • Trend filter: longs only while the frontier is green and rising, shorts only while it is red and falling.
  • Pullbacks: in a trend, a pullback to the frontier or the opening zone is a place to look for continuation.
  • Caution levels: the seller peak above price can act as resistance, the buyer peak below price as support.
  • Timing: late in the period the frontier is faster, so it sits closer to price.

Levels that are close together (inside the merge tolerance, measured in ATR) are merged into one zone. SignalWhat has to happen
