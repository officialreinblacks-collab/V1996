ICT PlaybookICT Imbalances [VHX]

ICT Playbook Indicator

Draws the full ICT story on your chart: liquidity gets taken, structure shifts, then a trade box with stop, target and the levels to watch.

Where to find it: Indicators → Trend → ICT Playbook. Tap the indicator's settings and the 📖 Read script button at the top of Inputs opens this page.

What it does

ICT Playbook looks for one specific story and draws every step of it so you can read it at a glance:

Price builds liquidity (a tight sideways box, or a clear swing high or low) → price grabs that liquidity and snaps back → the market makes a Market Structure Shift (MSS) → a trade box appears with entry, stop and target. Around that story it marks the gaps, order blocks and levels that usually matter next.

Both directions are covered. A sell setup starts with buyside liquidity taken above. A buy setup is the exact mirror and starts with sellside liquidity taken below.

Quick start

  1. Open a liquid market on 5m, 15m or 1h. Scalping and intraday timeframes suit it best.
  2. Add ICT Playbook from Indicators → Trend. By default it shows the latest 2 setups, so the chart stays clean.
  3. Wait for the MSS label and the red/green trade box. That is the moment the setup is complete.
  4. Read the extra levels (-FVG, -OB, -IOFED, HTF block) to decide where you want to enter. The box entry is the MSS close, the levels give you better prices if price comes back.
  5. Check the bigger picture on a higher timeframe before you act. The indicator shows structure, not the news.

How one setup is built

1 · Liquidity pool

Two things count as liquidity. A Liquidity Generation box: a sideways range at least Liquidity box min bars long and not taller than Liquidity box max height × ATR. And a swing high / low, found with Swing length bars on each side (you can turn swings off).

The pool line is labelled Buyside Liquidity (above, for sells) or Sellside Liquidity (below, for buys).

2 · Liquidity Grab

Price pushes through the pool by a meaningful amount, then a candle closes back inside within two candles. A small arc and the words Liquidity Grab mark the spike. The lowest point (or highest, for buys) of the build-up is marked as Accumulation Low / High.

3 · MSS (market structure shift)

Before the grab, the indicator remembers the last swing in the opposite direction. When a candle closes through that swing inside the MSS search window (and before price makes a new extreme beyond the grab), the dashed MSS line is drawn. If it never closes through in time, there is no setup.

4 · Trade box

Entry is the close of the MSS candle. Stop sits just beyond the grab extreme. Target is the risk multiplied by your Reward : risk (default 1:3). Red = risk, green = reward. The label shows 1:3 while the trade is open and adds TP or SL once price has hit one of them.

Show or hide parts

Each part of the drawing has its own switch: Liquidity generation box, Buyside / sellside liquidity line, Liquidity grab, MSS, Accumulation low / high, Inversion FVG, FVG levels, OB levels, IOFED levels, Breakaway gaps, Exhaustion gap, Trade box, HTF rejection block, and the Legend. Switch off what you do not need to keep a minimal chart.

Alerts

Alert: new setup (MSS) shows a banner such as “ICT Playbook · LONG setup (MSS) · BTCUSDT 15m” when a setup completes on the candle that just closed. Turn on Play a sound for an audible beep. Loading a new symbol or timeframe never fires old alerts.

Good to know

  • Setups are built from closed candles. The candle still forming is ignored, so a drawn setup does not vanish when it closes.
  • After a setup completes, the next setup on the same side has to start with a new liquidity grab after that MSS.
  • Only the latest setups are drawn. Raise Setups shown to bring older ones back.
  • Box height, exhaustion distance and stops are measured in ATR, so they scale by themselves between markets.
Important: this is a chart-reading aid, not a promise of profit. Every setup can fail and hit its stop. Test it on a demo or on past candles first and risk only what you can afford to lose.

ICT Imbalances [VHX] Indicator

Marks the places where price moved so fast that one side had little or no trading: Volume Imbalances, Gaps and Fair Value Gaps. Each box stays on the chart until price comes back and fills it.

Where to find it: Indicators → Trend → ICT · GAPS, Volume & Price Imbalances [VHX]. Tap the indicator's settings and the 📖 Read script button at the top of Inputs opens this page.

What it does

Traders watch these boxes because price often returns to them before moving on. The indicator finds five kinds of imbalance and draws each as a coloured box that stretches to the right while it is still unfilled:

MarkMeaning
VI+ / VI-Volume Imbalance. Two candles of the same colour whose bodies do not overlap, while their wicks still touch. The box is the empty space between the two bodies.
GAP+ / GAP-True gap. The new candle's low is above the last candle's high (or its high is below the last low). No trading happened in that zone at all.
FVG+ / FVG-Fair Value Gap. A three candle pattern where the first and third candle leave a hole that the middle candle did not cover.
LV+ / LV-Liquidity Void. Several FVGs in a row in the same direction, joined into one tall box.
Imp.FVG+ / Imp.FVG-Implied FVG. A softer gap built from the candle bodies and wicks when the three candles overlap a little.
I.FVG+ / I.FVG-Inverse FVG. An FVG that price closed through. It flips role: a broken bullish FVG now acts as resistance, a broken bearish FVG as support.
Dashed lineC.E. (Consequent Encroachment). The middle of the box, a level price often reacts to.

Green boxes are bullish (support below price). Red boxes are bearish (resistance above price). Gaps are sky blue and inverse FVGs are amber.

Quick start

  1. Open any market and add ICT Imbalances [VHX] from Indicators → Trend.
  2. Leave the defaults on first: Classic Volume Imbalances, True GAPs and Normal FVGs.
  3. Look for boxes that are still stretching to the right. Those are unfilled and still active.
  4. When price returns into a box, it lights up (highlight). Watch the dashed C.E. line for a reaction.
  5. When a candle closes through a box, it turns grey and stops. That imbalance is done.

How a box ends

Engulf (default)

A bullish box ends when a candle body closes below it. A bearish box ends when a body closes above it. Wick touches do not end the box, they only highlight it.

Mitigate

The box ends as soon as price touches it with a wick. The strictest and fastest way to retire a box.

Rebalance (FVG and GAP only)

The box shrinks as price fills it. Only the part that is still empty stays on the chart. When price crosses the whole box, it ends.

Box style

Border style and transparency, the highlight when price is inside a box, labels (size, colour, alignment) and the grey colour used for filled boxes. Every colour and every transparency can be changed. Higher transparency means a fainter box.

On a light chart theme, change Label colour to something dark so the words stay readable.

Good to know

  • Boxes are built from closed candles. A drawn box does not disappear when the candle closes.
  • Only the newest boxes of each kind are kept (the max settings). Older ones are removed from the chart.
  • Grey boxes are filled imbalances. Switch off Grey out mitigated boxes to keep their original colour instead.
  • Imbalances show where price moved fast. They are not a buy or sell signal on their own.
Important: this is a chart-reading aid, not a promise of profit. Price often ignores these boxes. Test on a demo or on past candles first and risk only what you can afford to lose.
