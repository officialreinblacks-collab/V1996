Volatility Accumulation Manager (VAM)

A portfolio and strategy calculator for spot crypto traders who buy in stages, take profit on strength or sideways moves, and track each entry separately. It is a calculator, not financial advice. It never says a lower price makes a buy safer, and it never places trades.

Link it to the site

<script src="vam-tool.js"></script>

<!-- any nav item, button or link -->
<a data-open-vam href="#vam">Accumulation Manager</a>

The tool opens as a full-screen panel above the page. It never touches the chart engine, so the existing charts keep running behind it. MPV_VAM.open(), .close() and #vam in the address also work. You can also load it inside start() next to tools-4.js; every name is wrapped in its own scope.

Optional settings, set before the script loads:

window.MPV_VAM_CONFIG = {
  navSelector: '#nav-tools',     // adds a nav item to this container
  fallbackButton: false,         // hide the small launcher once your nav item exists
  chart: () => ({coin:'BTC', tfSec:3600, candles}),   // feed the active chart
  getCandles: (coin, iv, limit) => Promise,          // use your own market-data service
  getPrice: coin => number,
  openChart: coin => {...},      // the "Open chart" button (also fires event mpv:open-chart)
  zIndex: 9000
};

Colours follow your theme if you define --vam-bg, --vam-panel, --vam-line, --vam-text, --vam-up, --vam-down, --vam-accent; otherwise a dark trading palette is used.

Where the data comes from

Trades: typed in by hand and stored in this browser only (Export / Import backup in Risk & Settings). Prices and candles: the active chart when it shows the same coin and timeframe, otherwise Binance public market-data endpoints (no key). A manual price can override any coin. No API keys are asked for; if you add account access later, use read-only keys with withdrawals disabled.

How the numbers work

Cost basis of an entry = amount + fee. Weighted average price = money spent on remaining coins ÷ remaining coins. Break-even = average cost incl. fees ÷ (1 − sell fee %). Target price (net mode) is the price where profit after buy and sell fees equals the target %. Exposure = invested cost ÷ (invested cost + cash). Portfolio value = cash + open positions at the current price; a coin with no price is valued at cost until a price loads.

Selling from the whole position lets you pick which entries the coins come from (oldest, highest-priced, lowest-priced, or proportional). Editing or deleting an entry recalculates everything that depends on it.

Risk rules

Rules warn before an entry is recorded or simulated: reserve, max entries, max exposure, max per buy, stop-adding when the position is down by your limit, minimum gap between entries, optional "needs a consolidation range" and "BTC bearish". Alerts such as drawdown, new low below the last swing low, recovery from a low, sideways range, volatility or volume spike are conditions, not signals. Averaging down adds money to a position that is already losing; losses can keep growing.

MTF Conflict & Trend Alignment

Reads 1D, 4H, 1H, 15m and 5m on their own, compares them, and ends in one decision: BUY, SELL or WAIT. It describes what price has already done. It is not financial advice and it never places trades.

Where to find it

Market Gauge (More → Market Gauge): the MTF alignment card sits under the Scan card. Strategies list: MTF Conflict & Alignment draws the same dashboard on the chart and writes the trade category next to every BUY / SELL arrow. The five timeframes are fetched separately, so it works on any chart timeframe.

The dashboard

TimeframeRole
1DMajor market direction
4HPrimary market structure and trend
1HIntermediate trend and confirmation
15mSetup development and confirmation
5mEntry timing and execution

Trend = Bullish / Bearish / Neutral from three votes: swing structure (HH/HL or LH/LL, double weight), EMA 20 vs EMA 50 with price vs EMA 50, and the direction of the last break of structure. Structure shows the last two swing highs and lows (HH/HL, LH/LL, or the mixed HH/LL and LH/HL). Signal = Aligned when that timeframe agrees with the dominant bias, Conflict when it opposes it, Neutral when it has no trend. The dominant bias is the 4H trend; if the 4H is neutral the 1D is used, then the 1H.

Conflict detection

SituationWhat the tool says
A · 4H and 1H bearish, 15m and 5m bullishCountertrend BUY setup. Reduce confidence and monitor major resistance.
B · 4H, 1H, 15m and 5m bullishTrend alignment detected. Wait for your entry confirmation and manage risk.
C · 4H bullish, 1H bearish, 15m bullish, 5m bearishTimeframe conflict detected. Direction is unclear. Avoid forcing entries.

The mirrored SELL cases work the same way. A fresh CHoCH on the 4H or 1D is reported as a structure reversal: reassess the bias.

Trade category (next to every BUY / SELL)

CategoryMeaning
TREND CONTINUATIONWith the dominant trend and the lower timeframes agree.
PULLBACK TRADEWith the dominant trend, after a temporary retracement on the 1H or 15m.
COUNTERTREND TRADEAgainst the dominant trend, without a confirmed change of structure.
REVERSAL SETUPAgainst the old trend, but a CHoCH plus lower-timeframe confirmation says structure has changed.

On the chart a ⚠ after the category means the trade is against the 4H or 1D.

Final decision logic

The tool does not reject every countertrend trade. It separates four cases: a trend-aligned continuation, a weak countertrend signal (WAIT), a confirmed short-term reversal (needs a lower-timeframe CHoCH, a held retest and at least 4 of the 5 core evidence items; confidence is capped at 58) and a major trend reversal (4H CHoCH confirmed by the 1H; capped at 65). When several timeframes conflict, or the 4H/1D structure just reversed against the idea, the answer is WAIT. A BUY or SELL always needs a fresh BOS on the 5m or 15m, and trades against the higher timeframe also need the retest.

Confidence

First the entry evidence is scored on its own: BOS / CHoCH (20), retest held (15), protected swing (10), volume expansion (12), liquidity sweep or room to the next 1H level (12), RSI support (8), entry-timeframe trend (7). Then alignment adjusts it:

AlignmentEffect
Strong (4+ timeframes agree)+8, only if the evidence already scores 50 or more
PartialNo change
Lower timeframes against the major trend-15 and a cap of 45 (58 if 4+ evidence items pass)
Several timeframes in conflictCap of 30, so WAIT
4H / 1D just reversed against the ideaCap of 45, reassess the bias

Alignment never raises confidence on its own. Labels: LOW under 40, REDUCED 40-54, MODERATE 55-69, HIGH 70+. A BUY or SELL needs 50 or more.

Higher-timeframe warning

When the idea opposes the 4H or 1D, a red box shows the conflict, the likely reversal zone (the nearest 4H / 1D swing level), the trade category, the confirmation still required (BOS, successful retest, protected swing) and the invalidation level. To check a trade before you take it, tap Check my BUY or Check my SELL in the Gauge card (or set Check a trade I am about to take in the strategy settings). Auto goes back to the tool's own read.

Liquidity Swings

Port of the TradingView indicator Liquidity Swings [LuxAlgo] (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, © LuxAlgo). It marks swing highs and swing lows, counts how many later candles touched each one, and shows the volume traded there. It describes what price has done; it gives no BUY / SELL signals and it is not financial advice.

Where to find it

Strategies list → Liquidity Swings. Turn it on for the intervals you want in its settings.

What you see

On the chartMeaning
Red level + zone (swing high)A pivot high. The zone runs from the high down to the top of the candle body (Wick Extremity) or the candle low (Full Range).
Green level + zone (swing low)A pivot low, mirrored.
Zone widthThe number of later candles that touched the zone. A wider zone means more touches.
Number on the levelThe volume traded by those touching candles.
Level runs rightNot swept yet. When a candle closes above a swing high (or below a swing low) the level stops there and turns dashed.
Pale box on the newest swingThe swing area of the latest pivot, visible until a close goes through it.

A pivot is only confirmed after Pivot lookback candles have closed on its right side, so a new swing appears with that delay and never moves afterwards.

Settings

SettingWhat it does
Pivot lookbackCandles on each side needed to confirm a swing (default 14). Smaller = more swings.
Swing areaWick Extremity or Full Range.
Filter areas by / Filter valueHide swings whose touch Count (or Volume) is not above the value. 0 shows every swing that was touched at least once.
Swings to showHow many of the newest swings per side are drawn (default 30).
Swing high / Swing low, coloursTurn each side on or off and change its colour.
Labels sizeTiny, Small or Normal.

Limits: it uses the last 3000 candles. The original script's Intrabar Precision (volume from a lower timeframe) is not included; volume comes from the chart candles. Losses are always possible.

Volume Delta Candles

Port of the TradingView indicator Volume Delta Candles [LuxAlgo] (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, © LuxAlgo). It looks inside each candle with lower-timeframe candles and shows whether buying or selling volume was bigger. It describes what happened; it gives no BUY / SELL signals and is not financial advice.

Where to find it

Strategies list → Volume Delta Candles. Turn it on for the intervals you want in its settings. Works for Binance symbols (it needs lower-timeframe candles).

How it is built

Every lower-timeframe candle inside a chart candle counts as buy volume if it closed above its open and as sell volume if it closed below. Delta = buy − sell. Delta % = delta ÷ the candle volume. The coloured bar grows from the bottom of the candle body when delta is positive and from the top when it is negative; the bigger the delta %, the longer the bar. In half bar mode the bar starts at the middle of the body.

What you see

On the chartMeaning
Green bar (Up / Down +)Positive delta: more buy volume than sell volume.
Red bar (Down / Up −)Negative delta. A bullish candle with a red bar (Up −) or a bearish candle with a green bar (Down +) means price and delta disagree.
Small horizontal lineClose price of the lower-timeframe candle with the most volume (on the side that won).
Dot in the next candlePrevious max volume price (optional).
Text at the top rightThe lower timeframe used.

Limits: the original's Ticks data source is not included (no tick history); only lower-timeframe data is used. Older candles than the number you set have no bar. Buy / sell is judged from each lower candle's colour, so it is an estimate, not true order-flow. Losses are always possible.
