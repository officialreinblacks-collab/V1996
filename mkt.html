Market watch

Is crypto falling or rising, and why?

Update for 3 November 2026

TL;DR

Crypto is falling. The move looks driven by institutional outflows, the Binance security shock, election and rate uncertainty, and a fresh build-up in leveraged positions.

The market has stabilized from its recent low, but the evidence still points to defensive positioning rather than a clean recovery.

Market breadth is weakening

Total crypto market capitalization fell 1.91% over the latest 24-hour window, from $2.62T to $2.57T. The broader market-size bundle shows a 2% decline over 24 hours and a 10.52% fall over 30 days.

The Altcoin Season Index is at 46, down from 48 yesterday, 54 last week, and 58 last month. Capital is rotating away from higher-beta altcoins.

Bitcoin dominance is 59.92%, compared with 60.02% yesterday and 60.03% last week. That slight decline does not offset the broader defensive signal from falling altcoin participation.

Institutional demand is the clearest pressure point

  • $643.2M of US spot Bitcoin ETF outflows on October 30.
  • Roughly $202M of net Bitcoin ETF withdrawals for the week.
  • $431.4M of Ethereum fund outflows over five consecutive trading days.
  • About $515M of net Bitcoin ETF withdrawals for October overall.

This weakens the main source of sustained spot demand that had previously supported the market.

The market is also digesting the reported $6.3B Binance hack and the customer withdrawals that followed, which has kept exchange and counterparty risk in focus.

Leverage is amplifying the move

Global open interest rose 25.35% over 24 hours to $365.29B. Average funding is positive at +0.0091502%, up 109.6% over 24 hours. Positive funding means longs are paying shorts, but here it also shows that leveraged bullish exposure is rebuilding while spot demand remains weak.

Bitcoin liquidations reached $88.83M over 24 hours, up 118.64%, adding forced selling to the risk-off pressure.

Rising open interest, positive funding, and falling market cap together are fragile: leverage is increasing faster than confirmed spot buying.

Macro and event risk are reinforcing the selloff

Crypto’s latest 24-hour return has a high correlation with QQQ (0.91916) and SPY (0.83773), using the available overlapping five-minute data. Gold rose 1.21% over the market-overview window while crypto fell, so risk appetite is splitting rather than lifting every asset class.

This week also brings:

  • The US midterm elections
  • Several central-bank decisions
  • Federal Reserve commentary
  • The October jobs report

Coverage says stronger labor data could reinforce expectations for a December rate increase, which could lift yields and the dollar and pressure Bitcoin. Weaker data could ease that pressure.

Bottom line

The evidence currently leans bearish. ETF withdrawals and leverage-driven volatility outweigh the recent rebound.

The read would improve if:

  • Institutional flows stabilize.
  • Leverage cools without another liquidation wave.
  • Total market cap begins recovering from the latest decline.
