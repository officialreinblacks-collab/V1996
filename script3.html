Higher Timeframe
Trend Frontier

A smooth trend line that lives inside each higher-timeframe candle, with the candle’s range, its opening zone, and a map of where buyers and sellers are most likely trapped.

What you see on the chart

  • Echo FrontierThe main line. It trails price inside the current higher-timeframe candle and restarts at that candle’s open. Its colour shows direction: green when it is rising, red when it is falling. The thick band behind it fades as the period ages.
  • Period dotA small dot where the frontier starts again after a new higher-timeframe candle opens.
  • HTF boxesOne shaded box per higher-timeframe candle, from its high to its low. Green means price is above that candle’s open, red means below.
  • High / low labelsPrice tags on the exact candle that made each period’s high and low.
  • ATR opening zoneA thin blue band centred on the period’s open, with a dotted line through the middle. Its half-width is a share of the previous higher-timeframe candle’s ATR.
  • Vertical dividersDotted lines where each new higher-timeframe period begins.
  • Trapped buyers / sellersA two-sided profile to the right of the last candle. Trapped buyers grow to the left, trapped sellers to the right. A blue frame marks the buyer peak and an orange frame marks the seller peak.
  • Status textA small line at the top of the chart: the higher timeframe, how far through the period you are, and the current frontier speed multiplier.

How it works

The frontier follows price, but not blindly

On every candle the frontier moves toward the close by a limited step. The step size is your Frontier Speed times the chart’s ATR(14), so a bigger speed hugs price and a smaller speed is smoother.

Echo memory

The previous higher-timeframe candle’s range (plus a quarter of it above and below) is cut into nine zones. Each time price sits in a zone, the tool looks at what price did three candles later and remembers it, with older memories fading over time. When price is back in a zone that has usually led higher, the frontier moves up a little faster than it moves down, and the other way round. Until enough history exists, the frontier simply moves evenly.

Late-period acceleration

As the higher-timeframe candle nears its close, the speed rises from 1x up to your final multiplier. It starts after the progress percentage you set and follows the curve you choose, so the frontier catches up with price before the period ends.

Trapped buyers and sellers

The three newest higher-timeframe candles, including the one still forming, are split into price rows. Each chart candle adds weight to the rows it touched, using its volume, its body and close position, and its wicks. Rows that are now under water count for more: buyers who bought above the current price, and sellers who sold below it. Red higher-timeframe candles lean toward trapped buyers and green ones toward trapped sellers. Older periods count a little less than the newest.

Estimate, not exchange data. The profile is built from candle open, high, low, close and volume. It is not real bid and ask delta, so treat it as a map of likely pressure.

How to use it

  1. Add the tool. Open Indicators, go to the Trend category and choose Higher Timeframe Trend Frontier.
  2. Pick a higher timeframe. It must be above the chart timeframe. On a 5m or 15m chart, 1h or 4h works well. If you pick one that is not higher, the chart shows a message telling you so.
  3. Read the frontier colour. Green and rising means buyers are in control inside this period. Red and falling means sellers are. A colour flip with price crossing the line is the first sign of a shift.
  4. Check the box and the open. Price holding above the period’s open and the opening zone is a stronger bullish read. Price stuck inside the zone means the period has not picked a side yet.
  5. Look right for the trap peaks. The blue frame (buyers) and orange frame (sellers) show where most stuck positions sit. Price often reacts when it returns to those rows.
  6. Wait for the close. The last candle is live, so the frontier and the profile can still move until it closes. Confirm with your own entry rules and stop loss.

Ways traders use it

  • Trend filter: only take longs while the frontier is green and rising, and shorts while it is red and falling.
  • Pullbacks: in a trend, price pulling back to the frontier or to the opening zone is a place to look for continuation.
  • Targets and caution levels: the seller peak above price can act as a resistance area, and the buyer peak below price as support.
  • Period timing: use the progress figure in the status text. Late in the period the frontier is faster, so its signals are closer to price.
Tip: keep the higher timeframe at least four times the chart timeframe so each period holds enough candles to draw a smooth frontier.

Good to know

  • Built from your chart’s candles. The higher-timeframe candles are made from the candles already loaded, so no extra data is requested. If the chart has only a few periods loaded, the first period is skipped and the opening-zone ATR uses as many periods as exist, up to 14.
  • Room on the right. The profile sits to the right of the last candle. The tool makes room once when you add it. If you scroll the chart back to the latest candle and the profile is cut off, scroll the chart left a little.
  • No volume, still works. On markets without volume, every candle is weighted equally and the status text says so.
  • Needs three periods. The profile appears once three higher-timeframe periods exist on the chart.
  • Live candle. The newest candle updates the frontier and the profile as it forms, and the values settle when it closes.
  • Not advice. This tool shows market context. It does not tell you to buy or sell. Always use a stop loss and size positions you can afford to lose.
