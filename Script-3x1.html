Adjustable Pro Plus Ranging Channel

A channel around price that gives BUY / SELL buttons at its edges, with optional Breakout, BOS / CHoCH and fair value gap tools.

A yellow dotted middle line is the best-fit (regression) line through the last candles. A green line sits above it and a red line below it, both at a set % distance. When price touches an edge and confirming candles follow, a tiny BUY or SELL button appears.

Set up

  1. Tap Strategies, open the Pro plus Metal category and tap Adjustable Pro Plus Ranging Channel.
  2. Pick a chart where price is moving sideways in a range. The tool is built for ranging markets.
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row (top left of the chart) to open its settings, tab Inputs.
  4. Set Channel width % so the green and red lines sit near the swing highs and lows you can see. Start with 3.28.
  5. Keep FIXED channel on. The lines stay put until you re-fit them. To re-fit later, tick Re-fit the fixed channel… once and press Ok.
  6. Wait for the BUY / SELL buttons. They only appear on closed candles.

How the signals work

BUY: a candle’s low touches the red line. That starts a setup. The BUY fires on the close of the 2nd green candle, counting from the touch candle. The greens do not have to be back to back.
SELL: the mirror. A candle’s high touches the green line, and the SELL fires on the close of the 2nd red candle.
  • By default the confirming candle’s body must sit fully inside the channel. This ignores spike candles that jump outside it.
  • If the confirmation does not complete within Max candles to wait (10), the setup is dropped.
  • After a signal, the next signal of the same side must wait Candles between BUYs / SELLs (20 by default), so repeated touches of one line do not stack.
  • If price leaves the channel and keeps going, turn on the optional Breakout marker below, or re-fit the channel.
  • BUY, SELL and Breakout buttons never sit on top of each other. If two would meet, the later one steps further out and its dotted line stretches to reach it.

Optional extras (all off by default)

Turn each one on in the tool’s settings, tab Inputs. They work on closed candles only, so nothing flickers.

1. Breakout. Switch on BREAKOUT. When price closes outside the channel for the set number of candles in a row (default 2, so the second candle confirms), a small dot appears on that candle with a dotted line to a tiny yellow Breakout button. An upper breakout puts the button above the candle, a lower breakout puts it below.
Candles between breakouts (default 20, try 20 to 50) is the wait before the next breakout on the same side can show. It stops retests of one spot from stacking buttons. One marker is drawn per move outside the channel.
2. BOS and CHoCH (market structure). Switch on MARKET STRUCTURE. The tool finds swing highs and lows. When a candle closes beyond the last swing, a dotted line is drawn from that swing to the breaking candle.
BOS (break of structure) = the break continues the current trend.
CHoCH (change of character) = the break goes against the current trend, a first sign of a flip.
Bullish lines are green and bearish lines are red, for both BOS and CHoCH. The label tells you which one it is. The very first break on the chart is labelled BOS because there is no earlier trend to flip.
3. Fair value gaps (FVG). Switch on FAIR VALUE GAPS. A bullish gap (light green box) is where a candle’s low sits above the high of the candle two bars before it. A bearish gap (light red box) is the mirror. Only gaps that price has not filled are kept, and the boxes stretch to the right edge while they stay open.
Gap counts as filled when price: Full (default) = price trades all the way through the gap. Touch = price enters the gap at all.
4. Filled gaps in grey. Switch on Also show the last FILLED gaps in grey and choose how many to show (for example 5, 7 or 20). A grey box runs from the gap to the candle that filled it, then stops. It is a quick way to see which gaps price has already visited.

Set up

  2. Choose a higher time frame for cleaner runs.
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open Inputs.

  • A doji keeps a qualified setup alive by default.
  • With signal on the Nth candle set above 1, a yellow prepare dot shows on each opposite candle before the signal.

Smart Money Concept (SMC)

Reads market structure, order blocks, liquidity and value zones straight on your chart.

SMC is built on one idea: big players move price in steps, and they leave footprints. This tool marks those footprints for you: structure breaks (BOS and CHoCH), order blocks, equal highs / lows, fair value gaps, previous day / week / month levels and premium / discount zones.

Set up

  1. Tap Indicators and open Smart Money Concept (SMC). You can find it with the search box or under Trend.
  2. Choose a higher time frame first (1H, 4H or daily). Structure is cleaner there.
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open its settings.
  4. Start with the defaults: swing and internal structure, internal order blocks and equal highs / lows are on.
  5. Turn on Premium / discount zones to see where price is expensive or cheap.
  6. Add one feature at a time. SMC can fill the chart quickly if everything is on.

What you will see

On the chartWhat it means
BOS (Break of Structure)Price closes beyond the last swing high or low in the direction of the trend. The trend is continuing.
CHoCH (Change of Character)Price closes beyond the last swing high or low against the trend. The first sign the trend may be changing.
Green / red labelsGreen is a bullish break (up), red is a bearish break (down).

Strong / Weak high and lowIn an uptrend the low is the Strong Low and the high is a Weak High (a likely target). In a downtrend it is the other way round.
Order blocks (boxes)The last candle area before a structure break. A bullish block is a possible demand zone, a bearish block a possible supply zone. A block disappears once price breaks through it (mitigated).
EQH / EQLTwo highs (or lows) at almost the same price. Orders pile up there, so price often sweeps them.
Fair value gaps (FVG)A three-candle gap price moved through fast. Price often comes back to fill part of it. Removed once filled.
Previous day / week / monthLines at the last period’s high and low. Common targets and reaction points.
Premium / equilibrium / discountThe swing range split into three: premium (top, expensive), grey equilibrium (middle), discount (bottom, cheap).

A simple way to read it

  1. Find the bias. On a higher time frame, look at the latest swing BOS / CHoCH. Up breaks mean a bullish bias, down breaks a bearish one.
  2. Wait for value. Bullish bias: look for price in the discount zone. Bearish bias: look in the premium zone.
  3. Look for a reaction at an order block or fair value gap inside that zone, ideally after a sweep of equal lows / highs.
  4. Confirm on a lower time frame. A CHoCH in your direction there is a stronger sign than a touch alone.
  5. Plan the exit. Targets can be the weak high / low, equal highs / lows or the previous day / week levels. A stop usually sits beyond the order block or the strong high / low.

Sweep MSS OB Setup

A step-by-step smart money setup: it waits for the whole chain to form, then signals when price returns to the order block.

The tool draws the setup as it builds (MSS, order block, sweep, IDM, BOS, target, accumulation) and prints a SELL or BUY only when price comes back into the order block and rejects it. It shows the stop loss and the target for you. Sell setups are shown below; buy setups are the exact mirror.

Set up

  1. Tap Strategies and open Sweep MSS OB Setup. You can also find it under Essential Trading.
  2. Pick a chart and a time frame. Higher time frames (15m and above) give cleaner chains.
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open its settings.
  4. Start with the defaults: direction Both, swing pivot length 5, sweep required.
  5. Scroll back on the chart. Past setups are drawn there, so you can see how the tool behaves on this market before you rely on it.
  6. Wait for the BUY / SELL button. Nothing prints until the whole chain is complete.

Price Action Wave Analysis

Reads the market as waves: swing highs and lows, supply and demand zones, and Head & Shoulders patterns.

The tool joins confirmed swing points with a zigzag and labels each one HH, HL, LH or LL. From that it finds the trend, marks demand and supply zones, and spots Head & Shoulders (and inverse) patterns with the neckline, the break and the retest.

Set up

  1. Tap Strategies and open Price Action Wave Analysis. It is also listed under Essential Trading and Price Action.
  2. Choose a time frame. 1H and above gives clearer waves.
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open its settings.
  4. Check Reverse signals first (see the box below). It is on by default.
  5. Choose Trade direction and Signals from (Both, Pattern only, or Zone only).
  6. Read the Wave trend badge at the top right of the chart to see the current trend.
Reverse signals is ON by default. With it on, the chart signals and the Head & Shoulders arrows point the opposite way to the classic pattern. For example, a break above an inverse Head & Shoulders neckline is shown as a down signal. Untick Reverse signals if you want the classic reading (demand zone or inverse H&S = BUY, supply zone or H&S = SELL). The text below describes the classic reading.

Reversal Probability Profile

A map of where price has turned around most often, drawn as a profile to the right of the chart.

The tool collects every confirmed swing high and swing low, groups them by price, and draws a profile on the right. A longer bar means price has reversed there more often. It also draws support and resistance lines from the pivots, each labelled with a percentage. It does not give BUY / SELL signals. It shows you the zones to watch.

Set up

  1. Tap Strategies and open Reversal Probability Profile.
  2. Pick a chart and time frame. The tool needs at least 60 candles on the chart.
  3. The profile appears to the right of the last candle. Pivot dots and lines appear on the chart.
  4. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open its settings.
  5. Start with the defaults. They look back 600 candles and use 5 clusters.
  6. If the chart is too busy, hide the pivot dots or cluster lines first (see Display settings).

  5. Watch for broken lines. A strong level that price closes through can flip its role.

Price Action Concepts

Market structure, volume order blocks and liquidity on one chart, with BUY / SELL signals.

The tool marks structure breaks (BOS, CHoCH and CHoCH+), order blocks with their buy / sell volume, equal highs and lows, trend line liquidity zones and liquidity grabs. A small Structure Scanner box in the top right shows the latest break. A BUY or SELL prints on an order block retest and / or on a CHoCH+.

Set up

  1. Tap Strategies and open Price Action Concepts.
  2. Pick a chart and time frame. At least 40 candles are needed.
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open its settings.
  4. Choose BUY / SELL signal from: Both, Order Block retest, or CHoCH+.
  5. Start with the defaults. Internal length is 5, swing length is 50.
  6. If the chart is crowded, switch off one group at a time (see Display settings).

  • A block disappears once it is mitigated (see Order block mitigation below). It cannot signal after that.

  1. Read the swing structure first. Is the latest break up or down? The scanner box tells you.
  2. Look for an order block in the direction of that break. A bigger volume share means a more important block.
  3. Wait for price to come back to the block and for the signal to print.
  4. Use liquidity zones, equal highs / lows and opposite blocks as targets. Put your stop beyond the block.

Market Structure Analysis

Reads the latest trend change and builds a full trade plan from it: zones, key levels, entry, stop and two targets.

The tool finds the most important swing before the market changed direction, then draws everything around it: premium and discount zones, the 50% level, CHoCH and BOS, the IDM, the liquidity sweep, open fair value gaps, the liquidity pool (SSL or BSL), and a Potential Trade Setup. A small panel lists the key levels and the bias. It analyses one setup at a time: the most recent one, bearish or bullish.

Set up

  1. Tap Strategies and open Market Structure Analysis. It is listed under Price Action.
  2. Pick a chart and a time frame. Higher time frames (1H and above) work best. At least 60 candles are needed.
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open its settings.
  4. Leave Direction on Auto. The tool picks the latest trend change on its own.
  5. Read the Key Levels · Trade Setup panel (top right by default) for the bias, key levels and trade plan.
  6. Turn on the BUY / SELL signal setting only if you want an arrow on the entry zone retest.

How the trade plan is built

  • Entry zone: the latest unfilled gap inside the move. Without one, the 50% to 61.8% retracement of the last leg.
  • Stop loss: beyond the entry zone, plus a buffer of 0.5 × ATR.
  • Take profit 1: at a reward : risk of 2 by default. If that would pass the low, the halfway point is used.
  • Take profit 2: the liquidity pool at the end of the move.
  • The bullish plan is the exact mirror.

How to use it

  1. Read the bias in the panel. Do not fight it without a reason.
  2. Wait for price to pull back into the entry zone, ideally inside the premium (for sells) or discount (for buys) side.
  3. Look for a reaction: a rejection candle, a lower-time-frame CHoCH, or the optional signal arrow.
  4. Place the stop beyond the zone, and aim for TP1, then TP2.
  5. Drop the idea if price closes beyond the stop level. The setup is no longer valid.

Support & Resistance (High Volume Boxes)

Draws support and resistance boxes only where strong volume backed a swing, then marks holds, retests and breaks.

The tool finds swing lows and highs on the close. A swing low only becomes a support box when strong buying volume shows up on the candle that confirms it. A swing high only becomes a resistance box when strong selling volume confirms it. Darker boxes mean heavier volume. The newest box of each type stretches to the right so you can watch how price reacts to it.

Set up

  1. Tap Indicators and open Support & Resistance (High Volume Boxes) [ChartPrime]. It is listed under Volume.
  2. Pick a chart that has real volume data. The tool needs it to build the boxes.
  3. Give it enough history. It needs at least 2 × Lookback + 3 candles (43 with the default).
  4. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open its settings, tab Inputs.
  5. Start with the defaults: Lookback 20, Delta volume filter 2, Box width 1.
  6. Watch the boxes at the right edge of the chart. They are the levels price is reacting to now.

How the boxes work

Support box: a swing low (on the close) is confirmed after Lookback candles. If the delta volume on that candle is strong on the buying side, a box is drawn from the swing close down by one ATR-based height.
Resistance box: the mirror. A swing high is confirmed, selling volume is strong, and the box is drawn from the swing close up.
  • Delta volume is the candle’s volume counted as positive when the candle closes up and negative when it closes down. A doji copies the last non-doji direction.
  • Box height is ATR(200) × Box width, so boxes scale with the market’s volatility.
  • Box colour strength compares the volume with the biggest volume of the last 25 candles. Stronger volume is darker.
  • The newest support box and newest resistance box keep extending to the right. At most 50 boxes are kept.

Retracement Trading Strategy

Waits for a pullback into the Fibonacci golden ratio zone, then prints BUY / SELL on the bounce back with the trend.

The tool finds a clear swing move (a leg), then waits for price to pull back into the golden ratio zone, between 38.2% and 78.6% of that move. When a candle bounces out of the zone in the trend direction, it prints a BUY (after an up move) or a SELL (after a down move). The chart stays clean by default: only the zone and the entry arrows are drawn.

Set up

  1. Tap Indicators and open Retracement Trading Strategy. It is listed under Trend.
  2. Pick a chart and time frame. Higher time frames (1H and above) give cleaner legs.
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open its settings, tab Inputs.
  4. Start with the defaults: Swing length 6, Min leg size 3 × ATR, zone 38.2 to 78.6, trade direction Both.
  5. Wait for price to pull back into the blue zone. The BUY / SELL arrow prints only after a bounce candle closes.
  6. Need more detail? Tick Stop-loss / take-profit lines, Extension targets or Show everything under SHOW ON CHART.

How the signals work

BUY: a swing low then a swing high form a leg that is at least 3 × ATR big. Price pulls back into the golden zone without falling below the leg start. A candle then closes green and above the previous candle’s high, with the close above the trend EMA.
SELL: the mirror. A down leg, a pullback up into the zone, then a red candle closing below the previous low and under the EMA.
  • Only one entry per leg. After it fires, the tool waits for the next leg.
  • If price does not reach the zone within Pullback must come within bars (60), or it breaks past the leg start, the setup is dropped.
  • By default the signal waits for the candle to close, so it does not repaint.
  • Stop-loss sits below the end of the zone (or the pullback low) plus 0.25 × ATR. Take-profit is the leg’s swing extreme. If that is too close, it uses a 2 : 1 reward to risk instead. A trade left open for 80 candles is closed.
  • The row under the tool name shows how many trades hit the target (TP) or the stop (SL).

Curved Trendlines

A curved channel fitted through recent swing highs and lows, with a live buy / sell volume profile beside it.

Markets rarely move in straight lines. This tool takes the last few swing highs and swing lows and fits a smooth curve (a parabola) through each set. The red upper curve is lifted so every swing high sits on or under it. The green lower curve is lowered so every swing low sits on or above it. Between them you get a curved channel with a gradient fill and a white mid line. It draws no BUY / SELL signals. It is a map of where price is curving.

Set up

  1. Tap Strategies and open Curved Trendlines.
  2. Pick a chart and a time frame. It needs real volume data for the profile, and enough candles (at least 2 × Pivot length + 5).
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open its settings, tab Inputs.
  4. Start with the defaults: Pivot length 10, Structure points 6, Extend bars 20.
  5. Read the channel: price near the green curve is at the lower edge, near the red curve is at the upper edge.
  6. Look at the profile on the right to see where buying or selling volume was heaviest.

How it works

  • Swing points: a high or low that is the extreme of Pivot length candles on each side (10). It is confirmed that many candles later.
  • Structure points: how many of the latest swing highs and lows are used for each curve (default 6, from 3 to 15).
  • Curve fit: each set is fitted with a least-squares quadratic curve, then shifted so it touches the furthest swing point.
  • Extend bars: the curves are projected forward (20). If they would cross each other, the channel stops just before that.
  • Live profile: volume from the earliest swing used up to the latest candle, split into buy and sell volume. Each candle’s volume is divided by where it closed in its range. The orange row is the POC, the price with the most volume.
  • Memory profile: volume from before that range, drawn above and below the live range where the channel has room. It shows older volume at those prices.
  • The curves can change when a new swing is confirmed, because the newest points join the fit.

% Retest Buy & Sell

Builds a percentage-sized box around a consolidation, waits for a break and a retest of its edge, then prints BUY / SELL.

The tool finds a sideways range, turns it into a box whose height is a percentage of price (default 2%), and watches its edges. The bottom edge is support and the top edge is resistance. When price breaks an edge, comes back to retest it, and then a confirmation candle closes, a signal is printed. Signals use closed candles only and never repaint.

Set up

  1. Open the tools picker and find % Retest Buy & Sell in the Premium Strategy category.
  2. Pick a chart and time frame with clear ranges. The tool needs enough history (default lookback is 200 candles).
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open its settings, tab Inputs.
  4. Set Range % (section B) so the box fits your market. 2% suits many charts. Try 1% to 5%.
  5. Leave Range Anchor on Auto and the Consolidation filter on.
  6. Watch the box go from dashed (developing) to solid (confirmed). Only a confirmed range can give signals.

How the signals work

1. Range. The highest high and lowest low of the lookback set the anchor. With a Low anchor the box runs from the low up by the Range %. With a High anchor it runs from the high down. A range must qualify for 2 bars before it is locked.
2. Break. A candle closes beyond an edge, plus a small buffer (0.1 × ATR).
3. Retest. Price comes back to touch the broken edge. The old resistance now acts as support, or old support as resistance.
4. Confirm. Within 3 candles of the retest, a candle closes beyond the edge, in the signal direction, and beyond the retest candle. That candle prints the signal.
  • Invert is ON by default. A break down that retests prints BUY, and a break up that retests prints SELL. Turn Invert off for the classic way: break up = BUY, break down = SELL.
  • Reclaim logic (optional): a failed break that closes back inside the box.
  • A zone is invalidated when price closes back inside it by 0.5 × the box height.
  • By default each zone gives 1 signal, with at least 3 candles between signals.
  • The forming candle is never used, so signals do not repaint.

Wyckoff Range Engine

Finds accumulation and distribution ranges, labels each Wyckoff event, and gives entries and targets.

The tool follows the classic Wyckoff story. A heavy-volume climax ends a trend, a rally or drop sets the range, and price tests the edges. When price finally breaks out of the range and pulls back to hold the breakout level, the tool prints the entry. A range after a selling climax is ACCUMULATION (green, looking for BUYs). A range after a buying climax is DISTRIBUTION (red, looking for SELLs).

Set up

  1. Tap Strategies and open Wyckoff Range Engine.
  2. Pick a chart with volume and a time frame where ranges are clear. The tool needs at least 60 candles. If a chart has no volume, it uses candle size instead.
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool’s row to open its settings, tab Inputs.
  4. Choose Trade Entry Signal. The default is LPS / LPSY, the safest entry.
  5. Leave the other settings on their defaults at first.
  6. Read the labels on the chart as the range develops: SC, AR, ST, Spring, SOS, LPS. Signals come only on closed candles.

UT / ShakeoutOptional small marks for pushes through the opposite edge that fail.

How the signals work

  • The range goes through Phase A to E. Dashed lines on the chart mark the phase changes.
  • The tool prints a signal at the close of the event you chose: LPS / LPSY (default), Spring / UTAD, SOS / SOW, or Spring + LPS.
  • A guide is drawn for the last entry: entry, stop-loss (just beyond the candle, with a 0.25 ATR buffer) and targets at 1, 2 and 3 times the risk (TP1 to TP3).
  • After the breakout a Target line is projected one range height beyond the range edge.
  • As a strategy, it also has the standard exit settings: take profit (default 4%) and stop loss (default 2%).
  • A range fails and turns grey when price breaks it decisively, it times out (400 candles), it turns out to be a trend, the breakout is lost, or no LPS appears in time.
  • Up to the last 4 ranges are shown.

Spike Counter Reversal

Counts runs of unusually large candles (spikes), then signals the reversal when the first opposite candle closes.

A spike is a candle much bigger than normal. When several spikes come in a row in the same direction, the move is often overextended. The tool counts them. Once the required number is reached, it waits for a candle in the opposite colour. When that candle closes, it prints a signal: BUY after bearish spikes, SELL after bullish spikes. It then draws a zone over the spike run.

Set up

A Fair Value Gap setup: a strong move leaves a gap, structure turns, a flag breaks, and the target is the gap fill.

A bullish gap gives the mirror, a SELL. Optionally, once the gap is filled and price rejects, an opposite signal can print because the original move continues.

  6. Optional: in the settings (⚙, tab Inputs), tap the blue Backtest report button to read the latest setup (entry, stop, targets, quality score and checklist) and the backtest on the candles loaded on your chart.

The three strategies

1 · Liquidity Sweep + BOS + FVG. Price wicks beyond a swing low and closes back above it (sell-side sweep). A strong bullish candle then closes above the previous swing high (BOS, or CHoCH if the trend was down). A fair value gap forms. When price returns into the gap and a bullish candle confirms, a BUY prints. Bearish is the mirror: buy-side sweep, bearish BOS, bearish FVG, SELL. A wick through the level is not enough; the candle body must close beyond it.
2 · Daily Open. The previous daily candle sets the bias: green means look for buys, red means look for sells. Buys are only looked for after price trades below the daily open (sells above). A sweep around that level, then a BOS close, gives the entry. The daily open and the previous open and close are drawn as lines.
3 · Fibonacci Golden Zone + FVG. In an uptrend (higher highs and higher lows) the tool measures the swing low to swing high and marks 0.618, 0.705 and 0.786. It waits for a bullish FVG that overlaps this golden zone, then a confirmed retrace into the overlap gives a BUY. Downtrends are the mirror. Targets: TP1 at 0.382, TP2 at the swing high, TP3 as an extension.
Confluence mode. A setup only prints when at least 2 (or 3) of the strategies agree on the same direction within the agreement window. More independent agreement raises the quality score. No single indicator alone creates a trade here.

How a trade is built

  1. Range: over the last 40 candles, a flat zone with at least 2 touches at each edge. By default only tight ranges count (not taller than 10 × ATR).

Trend Following · 200 EMA Pullback

Trade only in the direction of the 200 EMA, wait for the pullback, enter on a reversal candle. Found under Indicators, Trend. Made for the H4 and Daily charts.

Trend following means you let the market show its hand first. The 200 EMA gives the bias: price above it = look only for BUY, price below it = look only for SELL. You never chase the move. You wait for a pullback into the 38.2% - 50% Fibonacci zone of the last push, and you enter only when a Hammer or Engulfing candle confirms it at the 200 EMA and / or the broken swing level. The stop goes beyond the swing, the target at the next liquidity level, with at least 1 : 2 risk to reward. Signals appear only after the candle has closed and never repaint.

Set up

  1. Open Indicators, then Trend, and add Trend Following · 200 EMA Pullback (H4 / Daily).
  2. Use the 4 hour or 1 day chart. The tool needs enough candles for the EMA (about 240 with the default settings). If there are too few, a yellow note at the bottom says so.
  3. Open ⚙ to change the EMA period, the pullback zone, the candle patterns, the stop and the target.

The idea

  1. Structure. Every confirmed three-bar swing low (or high) on a watched time frame is remembered until it is broken.

Reinblacks Volume Profile & Auction Strategy

A rules-based strategy that combines the volume profile, auction shapes, volume analysis, candle confirmation and higher-timeframe context, with entry / stop / target plans, 1 % risk sizing, alerts, a dashboard and a built-in back-test. Found under Strategies.

The idea in one line. The tool builds a volume profile for each session (a 24-hour crypto day by default), finds the POC (most traded price), the Value Area (70 % of the volume: VAH top, VAL bottom) and the busy / quiet price zones (HVN / LVN). It reads the shape of the profile, then waits for a closed candle with volume and a candle pattern that confirm a trade at a meaningful level. It is a chart aid for paper trading first: nothing is guaranteed.
Honest limits. The volume on each price row is an approximation: every candle's volume is spread evenly over the rows between its high and low. It is not real bid / ask order flow. The app only holds the candles of your chart, so the lower timeframe is replaced by a "close must finish in the signal direction" check. All results are rule-based estimates.

Set up

  1. Open Indicators, then Strategies, and add Reinblacks Volume Profile & Auction Strategy. You can also find it with the search box.
  2. Use a liquid pair (BTC, ETH, SOL...) on 5m, 15m or 1h. The tool needs at least 80 closed candles.
  3. Tap the ⚙ on the tool's row. The defaults follow the original specification: 400 rows, 70 % value area, 1 % risk, minimum 1:2 reward to risk.
  4. Leave Higher timeframe on Auto (about 6 times your chart timeframe) or pick one yourself.
  5. Watch the dashboard and the back-test panel on a demo / paper account first. Do not trade real money from the first result.

Risk and exits

  • Size: units = (equity × risk %) ÷ stop distance, capped by the maximum leverage and rounded down to the quantity step. The dashboard shows units (linear / spot) or USD contracts (inverse). The profit and loss in the back-test are in quote currency for both types; the contract type only changes how the size is shown.
  • Stop: market structure (signal candle / last swing ± padding), ATR, or structure capped by ATR. A trade is skipped when the stop is wider than the maximum ATR or tighter than the minimum ATR.
  • Target: model target (ROT and RE) or a fixed R:R (default 2.0). A trade needs at least the minimum R:R (default 2.0), otherwise it is skipped.
  • Optional: partial profit, break-even stop and ATR trailing stop. Only one position is open at a time.
  • Fills: the signal appears when the candle closes and the trade is filled at the next candle open with your slippage and commission. If a candle touches both stop and target, the stop is counted first (worst case).

Checklist before going live
  • Run it on paper / demo for at least a few weeks and more than 100 trades before using real money.
  • Check several market phases (trend, range, high volatility) and several pairs, not only one.
  • Compare the signals with the real chart: the arrow must be on a closed candle and the entry at the next open.
  • Test with higher commission and slippage than you expect. If the result disappears, the edge is too thin.
  • Confirm the position size on your exchange (contract type, minimum size, leverage) before you place an order.

  • Never risk more than you can afford to lose.

Known limits

  • The profile is candle-based (volume spread over the high-low range), not true order flow.
  • The lower timeframe is a close-position proxy, because only the chart timeframe is loaded.
  • The back-test covers only the loaded candles, and results on past data do not predict the future.
  • The first session on the chart has no previous profile, so it cannot signal.
  • Exchange-specific rules (funding, liquidation, minimum order size) are not simulated.

Reaction vs acceptance. Reaction = price tests a level (VAH, VAL, POC zone), a rejection candle closes back away from it and the next candle confirms the direction. Acceptance = price closes beyond (or back inside) the level for the number of closes you set. A wick through a level is shown as "Wick only · no acceptance". Both appear in the engine panel and as alerts.
POC sequence filter. Two consecutive higher POCs = bullish, two lower = bearish. An opposite move is a warning (1/2); a second violation resets the bias to neutral / developing, and a fresh pair of moves is then needed. When the filter is on and "Block trades against a confirmed sequence" is on, S1 and S3 trades against the sequence are skipped (S2 is a mean-reversion setup and is not blocked).
Naked POCs. Every finished session leaves its POC. It stays untested (bold dashed red line) until a later candle trades through it, then it is marked tested. Nothing assumes price must come back to it. An untested POC in the trade direction can be the target (S3, and as a barrier for S1).
Stops and targets. Stops are placed beyond the cluster or swing plus padding, never on the POC / VAH / VAL, and are widened to at least the ATR protection distance. The target is placed a little before the next opposing barrier. If that leaves less than the minimum R:R (default 1:2) the trade is skipped. Size = 1 % of equity ÷ stop distance, capped by maximum leverage, quantity step and optional minimum quantity.

FVG Order Blocks (BigBeluga)

Finds Fair Value Gaps and draws an order block zone behind each one, so you can see where price may react when it comes back. Found under Indicators, Trend.

The idea in one line. A Fair Value Gap is a hole left by a fast move, where candle 1 and candle 3 do not overlap. The tool measures the gap in %, keeps only gaps bigger than your filter, and draws an order block (one ATR deep) at the edge of the gap. Green blocks are possible support, red blocks possible resistance. It is a chart aid: there are no automatic BUY / SELL trades.

Set up

  1. Open Indicators, then Trend, and add FVG Order Blocks [BigBeluga].
  2. Tap the ⚙ on the tool's row. The defaults are the original script's: filter 0.5 %, 6 blocks.
  3. Raise Filter Gaps by % to see only the big gaps, lower it to see more.
  4. Look at the green and red blocks to the right of the price. Wait for price to come back into a block and react before you act.
  5. Turn on Order Blocks Signals if you want a small arrow when price breaks back above a green block (or below a red block).
